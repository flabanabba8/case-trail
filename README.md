# Justia Case Research

Research same-litigation appeals and later treatment of a particular legal point with Justia opinion links and Hermes's configured web tools. No Lexis account is needed. Search separately by case name, reporter citation, and docket; the separate-party search can find appeals where the order of the parties changes, depending on the search provider. A result flagged `may_be_year_index` can be a directory of cases rather than an opinion; some cases are indexed under generic names, making docket searches useful. Then fetch the target and potential later opinions, including separate opinions. `justia_examine` locates references, nearby treatment cues and possible disposition passages elsewhere in the opinion for review. When more than 20 disposition cues match, it reports the first ten and last ten and flags the omission; all cues are unverified leads and may describe a dissent or another case. `justia_examine_file` accepts a locally saved UTF-8 opinion without sending the whole file in a tool call. `justia_review` checks exact quote offsets against provided text or local files and produces a proposed analysis with supporting and contrary discussion, extent, opinion role, source hashes and coverage limitations. Explicitly set `save` to retain a private receipt, then list or reopen it with `justia_reviews` and `justia_review_get`. `justia_review_check_sources` compares explicitly selected local files with a saved receipt and flags changed or unavailable text; matching text does not establish current legal status. Receipts contain case facts and short quotations and may be sensitive; the plugin does not save complete opinions. A receipt checksum detects accidental changes, not tampering or legal correctness.

A search query is sent to the configured Hermes search provider; Justia URLs are sent to its extraction provider. These providers may log requests. Search backends may ignore query operators, omit opinions, return outdated results, or fail. Optional jurisdiction and legal-issue hints add search phrases; they are not verified filters and can exclude relevant results. The extractor can return a partial document or only some opinions. A keyword, snippet, verified quote, or matching name does not establish case identity, legal treatment, binding effect, or whether an opinion is complete. A dissent is not a majority holding. A reversed judgment and overruling a precedent are different, and either may affect only part of a case. If provided, decision dates are checked for chronological order but are not authenticated; missing dates are explicitly marked unverified. Reviewer kind and reading scope are reported as supplied and do not prove who read what. Review the complete primary opinions, procedural history, court authority, publication and separate opinions. Verify reporter page citations against paginated sources and check intervening statutes and rules. No result does not establish that adverse authority does not exist. The plugin is a research aid, not a complete citator or legal advice.

Justia's [terms of service](https://www.justia.com/terms-of-service/) restrict commercial exploitation and reproduction of the service. Evaluate applicable terms before use or distribution; do not bundle Justia opinion content. Source text and search results are untrusted data, never instructions. Searches and fetches occur only when explicitly invoked; there is no background monitoring or paid API requirement from the plugin. Hermes web providers may have their own charges or access requirements. The configured extractor must support URL extraction: a search-only backend cannot fetch opinions. Respect site access restrictions; the plugin does not bypass them. A user can instead provide legitimately obtained opinion text to the examination and review tools.
